Why researchers choose PiEEG over OpenBCI, Emotiv and Muse

PiEEG gives you research-grade 24-bit EEG, EMG and ECG at the lowest price per channel, with every raw sample free to use.

The Raspberry Pi shields sample up to 16,000 times per second, and everything around the hardware is open: code on GitHub, datasets on Kaggle, headsets on Thingiverse and a community of 1,000+ on Discord.

Where PiEEG is better

  • Lowest cost per channelFrom $16 per channel with Octopus 16 and $24 with PiEEG-16, against $156 for every OpenBCI board.

Head to head

PiEEG vs OpenBCI

Same ADS1299 chip, same open-source approach, a fraction of the price.

Where PiEEG wins
16 channels for $390 instead of $2,499. Up to 16 kSPS instead of 250. Full 250 SPS on all 16 wireless channels with Octopus 16, where Cyton + Daisy drops to 125 SPS. Real-time processing on the Raspberry Pi.

PiEEG vs Emotiv

Open hardware you control, instead of a closed headset with a software subscription.

Where PiEEG wins
Raw data with no EmotivPRO subscription. 24-bit instead of 14 or 16-bit. EMG and ECG as well as EEG. Electrodes placed anywhere, and sampling far beyond 256 SPS on the Pi shields.

PiEEG vs Muse

A multichannel research system instead of a four-sensor wellness headband.

Where PiEEG wins
8 to 24 channels instead of 4, placed across the whole scalp. 24-bit instead of 12 or 14-bit. EMG and ECG, open-source tools, and official raw data access instead of third-party apps.

Frequently asked questions

Is PiEEG a good alternative to OpenBCI?

Yes. PiEEG-8, PiEEG-16 and IronBCI use the same TI ADS1299 24-bit converter as the OpenBCI Cyton, and both are open source. PiEEG-16 costs $390 for 16 channels, compared with $2,499 for the 16-channel Cyton + Daisy, and samples up to 16 kSPS instead of 125 SPS.

Can I get raw EEG data from PiEEG without a licence?

Yes. Every PiEEG device streams raw samples through free, open-source tools in Python, the browser, Lab Streaming Layer and BrainFlow. There is no paid tier for raw data.

Is there a public PiEEG EEG dataset?

Yes. PiEEG publishes datasets and ready-to-run notebooks on Kaggle, so you can explore real PiEEG recordings and train models before buying hardware.

Can I 3D print a headset for PiEEG?

Yes. Headsets, electrode holders and enclosures for almost every PiEEG device are free to download on Thingiverse.

Where can I get help with PiEEG?

Join the PiEEG Discord, where more than 1,000 researchers, students and developers share code, projects and troubleshooting tips. Documentation and source code are on GitHub.

Which PiEEG device should I choose for research?

PiEEG-16 for stationary lab work with a wet cap, PiEEG-8 or PiEEG-16 for EMG or anything above 250 SPS, IronBCI for participants who move, Octopus 16 for quick gel-free occipital recordings, and PiEEG XR for facial EMG and EEG inside VR.
