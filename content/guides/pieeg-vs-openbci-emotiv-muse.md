faqs:
  - question: "Is PiEEG a good alternative to OpenBCI?"
    answer: "Yes. PiEEG-8, PiEEG-16 and IronBCI use the same TI ADS1299 24-bit converter as the OpenBCI Cyton, and both are open source. PiEEG-16 costs $390 for 16 channels, compared with $2,499 for the 16-channel Cyton + Daisy, and samples up to 16 kSPS instead of 125 SPS."
  - question: "Can I get raw EEG data from PiEEG without a licence?"
    answer: "Yes. Every PiEEG device streams raw samples through free, open-source tools in Python, the browser, Lab Streaming Layer and BrainFlow. There is no paid tier for raw data."
  - question: "Is there a public PiEEG EEG dataset?"
    answer: "Yes. PiEEG publishes datasets and ready-to-run notebooks on Kaggle, so you can explore real PiEEG recordings and train models before buying hardware."
  - question: "Can I 3D print a headset for PiEEG?"
    answer: "Yes. Headsets, electrode holders and enclosures for almost every PiEEG device are free to download on Thingiverse."
  - question: "Where can I get help with PiEEG?"
    answer: "Join the PiEEG Discord, where more than 1,000 researchers, students and developers share code, projects and troubleshooting tips. Documentation and source code are on GitHub."
  - question: "Which PiEEG device should I choose for research?"
    answer: "PiEEG-16 for stationary lab work with a wet cap, PiEEG-8 or PiEEG-16 for EMG or anything above 250 SPS, IronBCI for participants who move, Octopus 16 for quick gel-free occipital recordings, and PiEEG XR for facial EMG and EEG inside VR."
